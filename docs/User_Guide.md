## 1. What This Tool Does

Clean Air & Climate Resilience shows a real-time snapshot of air pollution
risk for the Punjab-Haryana belt (and a second demonstration region,
Delhi-NCR) during stubble-burning season. It combines satellite data,
citizen photo reports, and real air quality monitoring stations into one
easy-to-read risk score, a short-term forecast, and a plain-language summary
of what's happening and what to do about it.

You do not need any technical knowledge to use it — everything is presented
in plain language on a single screen.

## 2. Accessing the Dashboard

Open this link in any web browser (desktop or mobile):

**https://clean-air-resilience.onrender.com**

No login, account, or installation is required.

**Note:** if the page takes 30–60 seconds to load on first visit, this is
normal — the free hosting service "wakes up" after periods of inactivity.
Subsequent loads will be fast.

## 3. Reading the Map

The map shows real air quality monitoring stations as colored dots:

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Green | Lower risk |
| 🟡 Yellow | Moderate risk |
| 🔴 Red | Higher risk |

Click on any dot to see the station's name and the current hotspot score.
You can zoom and pan the map using the `+`/`−` buttons or your
mouse/trackpad, just like any online map.

## 4. Understanding the Hotspot Score

The number shown under **"Hotspot Score"** (e.g. `0.427`) is a single risk
rating on a scale from **0 (lowest risk) to 1 (highest risk)**, calculated
by combining three real data sources:
- Satellite readings of atmospheric particles
- Citizen-reported photos of visible smog/haze
- Ground-station pollution measurements

A higher score means more urgent conditions. Below the score, a **"Trend"**
label tells you whether conditions are expected to improve ("declining") or
worsen ("rising") over the next few days.

## 5. Understanding the Forecast

The **"3-Day PM2.5 Forecast"** panel shows predicted pollution levels
(measured in micrograms per cubic meter, µg/m³) for each of the next three
days, based on recent real monitoring data. This gives you a short-term
outlook rather than just a snapshot of right now.

**For context:** lower numbers are better. Values above roughly 55 µg/m³
generally correspond to unhealthy air quality categories under most national
air quality standards; the exact health threshold varies by country/standard.

## 6. Understanding the Generated Alert

The **"Generated Alert"** panel contains a written summary, automatically
generated by AI (Google Gemini), explaining:
- The current risk level in plain language
- The most likely cause of the current conditions (e.g. seasonal
  agricultural burning)
- Recommended actions — both for residents and for local authorities

This text is written to be understandable without any technical background
— you don't need to interpret the raw score or forecast numbers yourself.

## 7. Switching Between Regions

Two buttons in the top-right of the dashboard let you switch between:
- **Punjab-Haryana** — the primary region, using real satellite and
  ground-station data throughout
- **Delhi-NCR** — a second region included to demonstrate that this system
  can work across multiple areas independently

Clicking either button re-centers the map and refreshes all the panels
(score, forecast, alert) for that region.

## 8. Refreshing Data

Click the **"Refresh"** button at the bottom of the side panel at any time
to re-fetch the latest computed score, forecast, and alert for the
currently selected region.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this official government data?**
The underlying data (satellite readings, ground-station measurements) comes
from real public sources — Copernicus (a European Space Agency program) and
OpenAQ (an open air-quality data platform that aggregates official
government monitoring stations). The hotspot score, forecast, and alert
text are generated by this project's own AI-based analysis, not issued by
any government body.

**Can I use this to make real health decisions?**
This tool is a demonstration/hackathon project intended to show how such a
system could work. It is not a certified public health advisory service.
For official guidance, consult your local pollution control board or public
health authority.

**Why does the Delhi-NCR region look different from Punjab-Haryana?**
Delhi-NCR is included as a demonstration of the system's ability to run
independently across multiple regions ("federated" design). Its underlying
satellite and citizen-report values are illustrative for this demo rather
than a fully live pull, which is disclosed in the project's technical
documentation.

**Why did the page take a while to load?**
The dashboard is hosted on a free-tier service that "sleeps" after periods
of no visitors and takes up to a minute to wake up on the first visit. This
is a hosting characteristic, not an error.

**Can I submit my own photo of smog/haze?**
Not yet in this version — live photo submission is planned for a future
version but isn't available on the current dashboard.
